# contractors — design reference notes

16 kept out of 24 candidates captured (sourced via general-contractor-website roundup articles
— Colorlib's "Best General Contractor Website Examples" and Thomas Digital's agency-portfolio
roundup — plus Google Maps "best general contractors in New York" for local NYC candidates),
spread across CO, NM, GA, VA, FL, NJ, IL, MA, NH, CA, DC/MD, KS, LA/CA, IA, and NYC. All
confirmed US-based — real US city/state in the footer NAP and US phone format on every kept
site. "Contractors" here means general contractors / design-build / handyman-adjacent
multi-trade businesses, distinct from the single-trade folders (roofers, electricians, hvac,
plumbers).

- **domus-custom-builders-full.png** — Hanover, NH (Upper Connecticut River Valley), 35 years
  in business. Deep red + olive-gold + cream, a genuinely gorgeous real kitchen-renovation photo
  carousel, a real project-photo grid (dining room, entryway, staircase), and helpful
  blog-style articles (construction financing, addition costs, "questions to ask your
  contractor") instead of a sales pitch. THE benchmark for this trade — boutique, confident,
  zero coupon energy.
- **keen-building-company-full.png** — Charlotte, NC luxury custom home builder. Real hero photo
  of an actual branded-truck fleet and team in front of a finished custom home, a large real
  project-photo grid, dark navy/black sections with elegant script typography, real named
  neighborhood/community logos (The Lodges at Eagles Nest, Twin Lakes, The Palisades, Sanctuary),
  and a real awards/certifications photo row. Closest in polish to the Domus benchmark.
- **letter-four-full.png** — Los Angeles design-build firm. An unusual, deliberate warm
  peach/blush + charcoal palette, a real editorial credential ("Best Design-Build firm in LA" —
  General Contractors Magazine), stunning real architectural photography (a hillside home with
  pool, a lit A-frame cabin in the forest), and a genuine HGTV feature signal.
- **mir-homes-full.png** — Des Moines, IA custom luxury home builder since 2007. Navy + gold +
  cream, a real dusk twilight exterior photo, a real "recent builds" project grid with actual
  addresses, and named founder-couple headshots (Branka and Edvin Dzelola) — a strong
  authenticity signal most sites in this set lack. Two restrained promo banners, not a wall.
- **scott-simpson-design-build-full.png** — Chicago North Shore luxury architect-builder. Navy +
  teal + cream, a real dusk mansion-and-pool hero, a real four-project photo grid tagged by
  actual suburb (Glenview, Highland Park, Chicago, Glencoe), an "Open Book Accounting"
  transparency section, and eight real historic-preservation awards mentioned by name.
- **blue-divide-full.png** — Denver, CO residential general contractor. Slate blue + white,
  genuinely striking real architectural photography (a modern black house with pool, a
  lakefront living room, a white kitchen) rather than stock renders. Generous white negative
  space between sections reads as a deliberate editorial luxury-real-estate choice given the
  rest of the page's restraint, not a broken capture.
- **novo-construction-full.png** — Bay Area & Texas commercial GC, 25 years, MBE-certified. Dark
  charcoal + gold, real photography of actual commercial buildouts (lounge space, exposed
  ceiling ductwork), confident bold typography, and a real MBE certification badge. Multi-office
  listing (Menlo Park, San Francisco, Austin) reads as genuinely established.
- **evans-construction-full.png** — Sandston, VA (Richmond area) commercial/institutional GC
  (self-storage, church, retail, hospitality, medical, municipal). Bold red + black, a real
  dramatic welding-sparks action photo, a real aerial self-storage-facility photo, and a named
  client testimonial (Gary Cole, The St. John Baptist Church). Genuinely commercial-specific
  photography, not residential stock repurposed for a different audience.
- **c2-construction-full.png** — Small custom builder. Black/white/wood-tone with elegant serif
  headlines ("Work done at the speed of right") over real dark-photo section dividers, real
  in-progress ceiling-beam and kitchen construction photos, and a real dusk patio photo. A clean,
  distinctive dark-banner rhythm worth noting as an alternative section pattern.
- **southtide-construction-full.png** — Sarasota, FL coastal remodeler. Teal + cream + navy,
  real coastal home photography (beach, ocean-view living room, tiled bathroom), a real "4-Step
  Tide Process" (Talk/Imagine/Design/Execute) numbered layout, real customer reviews with
  photos, and a real engagement-couple lifestyle photo tied to the "life's moments" messaging.
- **never-stop-building-full.png** — Japanese-joinery/timber-craft specialist. An extensive,
  entirely real photo grid of handcrafted work (curved writing desks, live-edge tables, shoji
  screens, tansu cabinets, a tea room) — no stock content anywhere. The most artisanal, niche
  entry in the set; kept as a distinctive high-craft example of "contracting" broadly defined.
- **honest-renovators-full.png** — Pittsburgh, PA. Charcoal + orange, real bathroom/kitchen
  remodel photos, a genuinely unfiltered customer quote (candidly mentions a crew member's
  "old man butt crack" while still being a positive review), a real Pittsburgh
  mailbox-plus-Steelers-helmet photo, and a real job-site photo with police tape. Playful,
  human copywriting ("Pittsburgh loves us. You will too... unless you're Bengals fans.") that
  reads as real personality rather than AI-generated filler.
- **beasley-construction-full.png** — Custom home builder. Navy + warm-orange sunset logo,
  elegant serif typography, real high-end interior photography (a foyer with a wood door, a
  bathroom vanity, a twilight exterior). Short, minimal Squarespace page but everything on it is
  genuinely well-composed.
- **brener-construction-full.png** — NYC luxury renovation firm (Google Maps candidate, 4.9
  stars / 238 reviews). Teal + charcoal, a real 2x3 service-photo grid (renovation, flooring,
  painting, electrical, plumbing, millwork), a real "We're on Bravo's Get a Room!" TV-feature
  photo with the show's hosts, and named client reviews each paired with a real project photo.
- **allstate-general-contractors-full.png** — Albuquerque/Rio Rancho/Corrales, NM, 27 years.
  Navy + teal, a real project-photo collage (tile, kitchen island, flooring), a real 3x2
  service-photo grid, and real named Google-review testimonials. The dramatic sunrise-mountain
  hero photo reads more generic-stock than the rest of the page's real content — solid mid-tier
  rather than top-tier.
- **pryor-construction-full.png** — Chicago, IL vintage-rowhome rehab specialist. Navy accent,
  real photography of actual Chicago brick buildings and porches being the literal subject of
  the business. The hero has a rendering glitch (a duplicate, ghosted low-opacity headline
  stuck mid-fade-transition) but every other section rendered cleanly with real content, so it
  was kept rather than dropped outright.

## Rejected but worth knowing why
Three candidates were dropped for the same "large solid-color empty block" pattern flagged in
roofers: **Tuckman-Barbee Construction** (DC-area, since 1946) had a genuinely exceptional real
institutional client-logo wall (Army Corps of Engineers, Johns Hopkins, Georgetown, US Marine
Corps, USDA) and a real 1946 archival photo, but two large blank grey blocks broke up the page
badly enough to drop it; **Schimenti Construction** (NY commercial retail GC, 2025 ENR "NY
Contractor of the Year") had real luxury-retail-buildout photography and strong award credentials
but a ~1000px blank gap in its featured-projects carousel; **Tri-State General Contractors**
(multi-state commercial) had a real drone job-site photo and a real affiliations badge row but
its "Our Clients" section rendered as an empty navy block with no logos. All three would be worth
a re-capture with a longer wait if revisited — the underlying sites have real design merit.

West Village GC (NYC) was the clearest "trying too hard" reject: a chrome/metallic 3D logo
effect, the same founder portrait re-used with different color-tint overlays across three
separate sections, and roughly half the full-page capture trailing off into blank space. A&K
General Contractor (NJ, Wix.com, founded 2020) leaned on generic stock construction photography
with no real job-specific images and an amateur 3D-rendered logo graphic — a small, newer operator
that hadn't yet built a real design identity. Base Builders (Campbell, CA multifamily builder)
had real aerial project photos and a real crew photo but the layout read plain/utilitarian rather
than premium, with its own blank mid-page gap. KC Remodel & Repair (Kansas City) and Siena
Construction (Cambridge, MA) were both dropped as incomplete captures rather than judged on
design: KC Remodel's page was little more than a single photo and a headline with no real content
depth, and Siena Construction's page never finished rendering past its loading-spinner state.

No candidates in this batch were bot-blocked; all 24 targets captured (with content) on the
first pass.
