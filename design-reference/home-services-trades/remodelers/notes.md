# remodelers — design reference notes

15 kept out of 22 candidates captured (1 pre-existing: letter-four; 21 new candidates sourced
from a curated "best remodeling company website" agency roundup of real client sites, spread
across VA, MN, MD, CA, TX, PA, MI, IL, DC, and MA). Sourcing was open to any real business
worldwide with a genuine, premium, hand-built site — quality and design discipline were the
bar, not geography — though every kept site in this trade happened to be US-based.

- **san-francisco-design-build-full.png** — San Francisco, CA. THE benchmark of this set.
  Minimalist editorial layout, magazine-quality real interior photography, a distinctive
  split "Reviews" / "Projects" image-link section, a named client quote (J. Kuwamoto), real CA
  contractor license number, and a design-credit line ("Brand Identity by Flight Design").
  Boutique, restrained, nothing performative.
- **united-elite-group-full.png** — New York City. Black/white with gold accents, real luxury
  interior renovation photography, a real Manhattan building exterior shot, named client
  testimonials via Houzz/Google (Martha C., Jose Powell, Delbert J. Moffitt), and a genuine
  award-badge row (AIA, CRAN, Best of Houzz 2018/2020/2021/2022) that reads earned. High-end
  NYC renovation-contractor identity, close behind SF Design Build in polish.
- **case-architects-remodelers-full.png** — Washington DC region, 65+ years in business. Red +
  white, a named client testimonial (Janet S.), real hand-drawn floor plans and CAD site plans
  (a genuine, unique authenticity signal), four real design-studio locations with real interior
  photos, a real "our work" project grid split by room type, and a blog. Deep, credible content.
- **mcdonald-remodeling-full.png** — Minneapolis/St. Paul, MN. Navy/blue system, real interior
  photos, a video-testimonial thumbnail, an extensive real award-logo row (NARI, ROMA, design
  awards), FAQ section, and a named client quote. Strong editorial feel throughout a long page.
- **kurtis-kitchen-bath-full.png** — Southeast Michigan (Clarkston/Livonia/Royal Oak/Utica), 55
  years in business. Red/white, a real family photo, real project photos, an extensive review-
  platform badge grid (Google 5.0, Facebook 4.3, Angi 4.3, BBB A+), and a real multi-pin service
  map with named locations.
- **degnan-design-build-remodel-full.png** — Windsor, WI (Madison area). Red/white/gold, a real
  4-photo project grid, a real 10-person team photo, and NARI/CotY award badges. Warm, direct,
  genuinely small-business.
- **south-bay-design-center-full.png** — Lomita, CA (South Bay LA — Palos Verdes/Redondo Beach).
  Neutral warm palette, an 8-photo real showroom-quality portfolio grid labeled by real
  neighborhood, and a real 2021 Kitchen & Bath Design Awards Gold Winner badge. Premium
  showroom photography throughout.
- **512-remodel-full.png** — Austin, TX. Bold blue/black typography ("DETAIL. CRAFTSMANSHIP.
  QUALITY."), real before/after bathroom project photos, a real crew photo at a Habitat for
  Humanity build (genuine community give-back, not staged), and NARI badges.
- **mint-construction-full.png** — Harrisonburg, VA (Shenandoah Valley). Green/gray, a real
  showroom video hero, a stat block (100+ bathrooms, 60+ kitchens, etc.), a real latest-projects
  gallery, and named 5-star reviews (Brad Cohen, Sarah Corry, Michael Mirello).
- **friendly-structures-full.png** — Blacksburg, VA. Sage green/navy, a real founder-family
  photo (Meredith & Derick with their kids), a real mountain-landscape hero, and wood-texture
  accent panels. Genuine husband-and-wife boutique design-build identity.
- **at-design-remodel-full.png** — Cape Cod, MA. Blue/white/green minimal system, a real founder
  photo ("Meet John"), real kitchen/bath project photos, and a named 5-star client review
  (Millie Long, West Dennis MA). Restrained, small-business, real.
- **euro-design-remodel-full.png** — Baltimore–Washington DC metro (Woodbine, MD). Blue system,
  a real 6-project bathroom-remodel photo grid with locations, and a review-platform badge row
  (Google/Yelp/HomeAdvisor/Houzz). The testimonial-card text is partially blurred/illegible in
  this capture — a minor rendering quirk — but the rest of the page is solid enough to keep.
- **hdr-remodeling-full.png** — Bay Area, CA. Clean green/white/orange, real kitchen/bath
  project photos, named review quotes across three platforms, and a live Instagram grid. A
  couple of generous whitespace gaps mid-page keep this from the top tier, but the real content
  present is strong.
- **honest-renovators-full.png** — Pittsburgh, PA. Dark navy/orange, a distinctly personal named
  testimonial with real voice/humor (Chris Edwards), real crew and job-site photos, and a blog.
  Rougher production values than the top tier but genuinely authentic personality — a good
  counter-example to the polished-corporate norm.

Also kept (already present before this pass): **letter-four-full.png**.

## Rejected but worth knowing why
**stellar-remodeling-design** and **normandy-remodeling** both had real, credible underlying
businesses (an established Charlottesville brand with a "seen on TV" credential; a
Chicagoland firm since 1979) but the full-page captures came back roughly half blank —
large solid-color gaps between sections consistent with a lazy-load/scroll-animation timing
failure rather than an actual design flaw. Worth a re-capture with a longer settle time if
revisited. **imagineer-remodeling** and **eco-general-contractors** were real, well-produced
sites but turned out to be a fire/water restoration company and a commercial general
contractor respectively once inspected closely — a category mismatch for a residential
remodeler folder, so both were dropped regardless of quality. **renovation-husbands** is a
genuinely well-produced site but reads as a media/personal-brand property (a "Battle on the
Mountain" streaming show, blog, shop links) rather than a lead-gen service business — no
clear service pages or quote CTA, so it wouldn't be useful as a redesign reference. **kc-
remodel-repair** was too thin to be useful — essentially a single stylized photo and a
tagline with no real page depth.

One candidate was bot-blocked and not retried since the trade already had a healthy count:
**luxe-kitchen-bath** (403 Forbidden). The pattern of Cloudflare/anti-bot walls and dead
domains recurred far more heavily in the restoration trade sourced in this same pass.
