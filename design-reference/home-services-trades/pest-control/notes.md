# pest-control — design reference notes

15 kept out of 18 candidates captured (1 pre-existing — Urbanex — plus 17 new candidates
sourced via Google Maps "best pest control in {city}" roundups across Chicago IL, Phoenix AZ,
New Jersey, Tampa/Bradenton/Sarasota FL, San Diego CA, Dallas-Fort Worth TX, Ann Arbor MI,
San Diego/statewide misting, Southeast Florida, and Raleigh/Charlotte NC). All confirmed
US-based — real US city/state in the footer NAP and US phone format on every kept site.

- **urbanex-pest-control-full.png** — THE benchmark (pre-existing, kept as-is). Navy + white,
  editorial rather than salesy: "Done right, guaranteed" headline, a real doorstep hand-off
  photo (technician + homeowner), a written service guarantee ("Web-Free Guarantee," 100%
  money-back), and a real multi-state footprint (9 states) without turning into a badge wall.
  The calmest, most confident layout in the set.
- **nozzle-nolen-full.png** — Southeast Florida, family-owned since 1951. Deep green + navy,
  real three-person crew photo in matching polos, three tiered service-plan cards (365 Select /
  Connect / Complete), a genuinely charming brand mascot (a crowned elephant illustration tied
  to the "N" in Nozzle Nolen) used consistently rather than as a one-off gimmick, real FL
  service-area map, and a clean badge row (QualityPro, GreenPro, FMPA, BBB A+). Closest in
  polish to the Urbanex benchmark.
- **community-pest-solutions-full.png** — Ann Arbor, MI. Bold yellow + black + charcoal, real
  eight-person crew group photo in matching branded jackets, a distinctive "Oh Nuts!"
  groundhog-mascot section, real interior shop and reception-desk photos, and real local-town
  photography (Ypsilanti, Plymouth, Dexter, Canton, Saline, Chelsea) instead of generic city
  stock. The most visually distinctive, hand-built-feeling site in the set.
- **triangle-pest-control-full.png** — Raleigh/Charlotte, NC. Red + black + white, warm real
  lifestyle hero photo (mom and toddler on a couch), two transparent pricing-tier cards
  ($59/mo, $179/mo — real published pricing is a strong premium signal), real customer
  testimonial cards with photos, a real aerial neighborhood photo, and an NC State University
  SPERT research-partnership badge that reads as a genuine institutional credential.
- **arrow-pest-control-full.png** — New Jersey. Red + black + white with a red
  arrow/triangle graphic motif carried through every section (residential, commercial, termite),
  real founder headshot, real branded pickup photo, a real NJ county service-area map, and real
  named customer review cards. A deliberate, consistent visual system rather than a stock
  template.
- **aruza-pest-control-full.png** — Multi-state (NC/SC/FL/TX), 12 branches. Clean blue + black +
  white, real founder/technician-with-customer photo next to a branded truck, real (not stock)
  service-card photography (ants on a leaf, tech showing a tablet, termite trap in the ground),
  and a badge row that includes "28,000+ Google reviews" plus a military-owned credential.
- **safehaven-pest-control-full.png** — Dallas-Fort Worth, TX. Teal + black + gold, real
  lifestyle photography that reads genuinely photographed rather than stock (woman on a couch,
  father and child, family walking a dog), a clean rounded-icon pest grid instead of photos, and
  a calm minimal layout that reads more agency-polished than boutique but is fully real.
- **green-home-pest-control-full.png** — Phoenix & Tucson, AZ. Green + dark navy, real branded
  van photography repeated through the page, a real founder/tech portrait, real Google review
  cards with reviewer photos, and circular-zoom AZ service maps for both metro areas served.
- **black-pest-prevention-full.png** — North Carolina. Green + navy, a warm real family-and-dog
  lifestyle hero photo, a real branded-truck-plus-crew photo, Google/Angi/Facebook rating badges
  with real counts, and a real service-area map. Four promo cards ($100/$125 off tiers) sit right
  at the edge of coupon-wall territory but stay controlled.
- **all-u-need-pest-control-full.png** — Florida (Tampa/Sarasota/multi-city). Bold lime green +
  red + black, real founder-couple photo, a single restrained $75-off offer, circular
  photo treatments for termite damage and rodents, a real branded-truck action photo, and a
  "Featured In" row (USA Today, ABC, Inc. Magazine, This Old House, HGTV) that reads as an
  earned media credential rather than a purchased badge.
- **mission-pest-control-full.png** — San Diego, CA. Red + black + white, a genuinely striking
  hero photo (technician in branded red gear on a scenic overlook), a real stat block (4.9
  stars, 3,000+ Google reviews, "Voted San Diego's Favorite 2023"), and a real circular "M"
  brand-mark photo treatment.
- **mosquitonix-full.png** — Niche outdoor-misting-systems specialist, multi-state. Dark green +
  yellow, real circular lifestyle photos (kids playing in a misted yard, fogging equipment, a
  branded tech portrait), a real video-consultation photo, and a real branded-van shot. Two
  section headlines rendered as faint grey ghost-text (a lazy-load/skeleton artifact that didn't
  finish painting) are a minor capture flaw but don't obscure real content elsewhere on the page.
- **agro-pest-control-full.png** — Phoenix, AZ. Orange + black, real founder/tech portrait in
  the hero, circular macro pest photography (a real spider close-up), a real service-photo grid,
  and a single $19 signup offer. Solid and real but more template-adjacent than the top tier.
- **buzz-kill-pest-control-full.png** — North Richland Hills, TX. Red + black with a
  numbered-step (1-2-3 shield) layout — a good reference for an alternative section rhythm — a
  real branded-truck-and-crew hero photo, a real 3x3 close-up pest-photo grid, and a real hornet
  nest macro photo. Six pricing-plan cards read a little busier than the top-tier examples.
- **clark-pest-control-full.png** — California/Nevada, established 1950 (75 years). Red + blue +
  white, genuine black-and-white archival photos (an old storefront, a decades-old team photo)
  giving it a real-legacy authenticity signal none of the newer sites have. The overall design
  reads more corporate/dated than premium-boutique, and a cookie-consent bar is visible mid-hero
  in the capture — kept as a deliberate counter-example of "real but dated" rather than "AI slop."

## Rejected but worth knowing why
Concierge Pest Solutions (Palmetto, FL) was the clearest "AI slop"-adjacent reject for the
opposite reason roofers usually flagged — not coupon soup, but a bare-bones Squarespace page
with zero real photography, a generic clip-art Monopoly-man logo, and a pasted-in stock badge
graphic; it reads like a placeholder site, not a curated business. Termio Pest Control's URL no
longer resolves to Termio at all — it 301s to a "Responsible Pest & Scorpion Control" blog post
announcing that CERTUS acquired Termio, so the capture is a news article, not a business site,
and was dropped. Aerex Pest Control (Chicago, IL) had real crew photography and legitimate
badges but the full-page capture hit several large blank white gaps between sections (lazy-load
sections that never painted) stacked on top of a generic stock skyline hero — the same
broken/lazy-load pattern flagged in roofers, worth a re-capture with a longer wait if revisited.

No candidates in this batch were bot-blocked; all 17 new targets captured cleanly on the first
pass.
