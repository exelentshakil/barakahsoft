# hvac — design reference notes

17 kept out of 67 candidates captured (sourced via roundup articles + agency portfolio mining;
this trade started from zero — no pre-existing screenshots). Real businesses only. Geography was
not a filter — the brief is to find the best-designed real sites regardless of country — so this
set includes one strong Canadian find alongside a majority of US businesses spanning CA, TX, IL,
MA, AL, NM/GTA, TN, WA, MN, and AZ. This was the hardest trade to source clean keepers from: the
HVAC space is saturated with "$X off tune-up" coupon-card rows and cartoon mascots (see rejects
below), so the keep rate here (~25%) was noticeably lower than electricians or plumbers.

## Rejected but worth knowing why
Cartoon mascots were rampant in this trade, more than in electricians or plumbers: Williams
Mechanical's cartoon plumber character, Goettl's cartoon flashlight-boy logo (paired with a
"$299/$499/$50 Off" coupon row), Bradham Brothers' cartoon illustrated spokesperson (paired with
a "$500 Off" hero discount), Service Wizard AC's wizard-hat mascot, Hiller's smiley-face mascot
plastered on vans and membership cards, and DASH's running-man icon. Explicit coupon/discount
card rows were even more common than mascots: Just Right Heating & Cooling, A.B. May, Peak
Heating & Cooling, Roby Services, Same Day Heating & Air, Genz-Ryan, Complete Climate Services,
Trademark Heating & Air (despite an otherwise bold hot-pink/navy palette), Grove Heating &
Cooling (a literal photo of scissors cutting a coupon), Sanborn's, and Command Comfort all had
2-4 dollar-off cards front and center. Alexander's Heating and Davis Air Conditioning leaned on a
dated red/blue swoosh aesthetic and thin, mostly-product-shot photography rather than real
crew/jobsite work. Tip Top HVAC and Alpine Heating & Air were the clearest template-demo tells —
Tip Top used obvious stock-photo models as "technicians" and a UPS-style suite address, while
Alpine's page still had unfilled placeholder text ("[Your Name/Company Name]") and completely
unrelated stock photography (leather-bound books, a beach sunset) in place of real content.
GreenSavers, Estes Services, Boston Standard Company, Bardi, and Service Champions all had
real, promising elements (an ENERGY STAR Contractor-of-the-Year award, a historic founder photo,
genuine crew photography) undercut by extensive broken/blank lazy-load sections — in Bardi's
case, more than half the page was empty. The Cooling Company and Eco Electric, Plumbing, Heating
& Air were both extremely long, text-dense SEO content pages that read as content-marketing
templates rather than curated design. West Coast Heating, Air Conditioning & Solar was rejected
for an overloaded, cluttered pricing-tier and promotion layout. NRG Heating & Air turned out to
be the wrong company entirely (redirects to NRG Energy, a national utility retailer, not an HVAC
contractor).

Several candidates were bot-blocked (Cloudflare/JS challenge or 403) and not retried given the
trade's already-thin yield: Evolution Mechanical, Jacobs Heating & Air, DALCO Heating & Air, Gee!
Heating and Air, Travis Crawford HVAC, Rusk Heating and Cooling, and MechaniCool. Mission AC &
Plumbing and Smock HVAC both produced broken/washed-out or overlay-obscured captures and were
dropped. Skylake Heating & Air's domain had expired. Comfort Monster and Village Plumbing & Air
failed to resolve/timed out and were not retried.
