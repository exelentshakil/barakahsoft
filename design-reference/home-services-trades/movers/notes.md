# movers — design reference notes

21 kept out of 28 candidates captured (sourced via Colorlib's "30 Best Moving Company Website
Examples" agency-curated roundup, Google Maps "best movers in {city}" for NYC and Chicago, and
a targeted search for premium international movers), spread across NM, MA, CA, TX, VA, FL, NY,
IL, IA/multi-state, and London, UK. Geography was NOT used as a filter for this trade (per
product-owner direction mid-task) — the goal was the best, most premium real sites regardless of
country, so one UK luxury mover made the cut alongside the US majority. Quality bar unchanged:
real businesses, real photography, real design discipline, no coupon-wall/generic-stock/dated-
template "AI slop."

- **greystone-relocation-full.png** — THE benchmark. An ultra-luxury white-glove relocation
  concierge (not a standard mover) in navy + cream + white, a numbered 01-04 "How It Works"
  process, real photography of packing/home-setup/specialty-storage/wine-cellar services, and a
  real client-logo wall (HBO, LA Clippers, Lucky Brand, Apple, Dyson, West Elm, WeWork) backed by
  named celebrity/public-figure testimonials (comedian Sarah Silverman, Super Bowl champion Brock
  Osweiler, True Religion co-founder Kym Gold). Nothing else in the set matches this level of
  editorial restraint plus verifiable high-profile social proof.
- **williams-and-yates-full.png** — London, UK, tailor-made luxury moving and fine-art logistics.
  Charcoal + blush + cream, a real branded-truck-at-a-country-manor hero, real photography of a
  staff member measuring artwork and packing museum-grade ceramics, British Association of
  Removers (BAR) accreditation, and real photos of international crating jobs (a superyacht
  loading dock, a European hillside village). The strongest international pick — matches
  Greystone's tier of premium restraint.
- **roadway-moving-full.png** — NYC, 4.9 stars / 4,318+ reviews. Purple + pink + blue, real
  branded-truck-wrap photography throughout, a real "Official Moving and Storage Partner of the
  New York Yankees" partnership, real community/pride-parade photos, and a real Google/Yelp/
  Trustpilot/Angi badge row. Confident, consistent, and clearly a real established local brand.
- **piece-of-cake-moving-full.png** — NYC, 4.8 stars / 13,945 reviews (highest review count in the
  set). Bold, unapologetic hot pink carried through every section — real branded-box photography,
  a real "Official Moving & Storage Partner of the New York Knicks" credential, a real branded
  mascot ("Pinky the Truck") caught at a real parade, and real named customer testimonial cards.
  The most consistently confident single-color brand system in the batch.
- **new-city-moving-full.png** — Chicago, IL, since 2009. Navy + red, extensive real
  Chicago-specific photography (a branded truck under an underpass, a real brownstone packing
  job, a real video-testimonial thumbnail), a real interactive-style Chicago service-area map,

## Rejected but worth knowing why
Four candidates were dropped for the same "broken/blank capture" pattern flagged repeatedly in
roofers and contractors: **The Professionals Moving Specialists** (Chicago) rendered as roughly
70% empty white space with an unreadable navy footer (text the same color as its background);
**Bold Moving & Storage** (Richmond, VA) was the clearest full-site break — multiple grey
placeholder blocks with no images, an unstyled wall of raw customer-review text with no card
formatting, and empty avatar circles; **Careful Movers Inc.** returned a raw WordPress "critical
error" page instead of a site; **Dumbo Moving and Storage** (Brooklyn) was otherwise one of the
richest captures in the set (real five-borough photography, a full moving-day checklist, real
resource articles) but its testimonial-cards row rendered as three empty bordered boxes with no
content, which was enough to drop it given how central testimonials are to the trade's
authenticity signal. Move-tastic (Chicago) wasn't broken, just thin — a warm founder-origin story
(Pete and Pat, friends since high school) but almost entirely icon-and-text sections with only one
real photograph on the whole page, which reads more like a lead-gen landing page than a curated
premium design. FlatRate Moving (NYC's self-described "best moving company," 500K+ moves) had a
strong opening (real 5-star review carousel, real trust badges) but the capture broke down into a
majority-blank page for the remaining two-thirds of its length — worth a re-capture with a longer
wait if revisited given the brand's scale.

Lux Moves (Sydney/Melbourne/Gold Coast, Australia) was the only bot-blocked candidate — it
returned a styled "403 Forbidden" page rather than the real site and was not retried, since the
trade already had a healthy surplus without it.
