# solo-service-provider — design reference notes

12 kept out of 16 candidates captured (personal trainers, wellness/health coaches, business
coaches, a runway coach, and two therapists). This is the category where "premium" most often
means confident personal-brand photography rather than complex layout — worth remembering when
hand-building this vertical's section variants.

- **ultimate-performance-full.png** — The best in the folder. Burgundy/red brand color applied
  with total discipline (hero, stat callouts, CTAs, review cards, map pins), a celebrity press
  quote used as a full-bleed pull-quote section, real diverse client transformation photos (not
  posed stock), a world map of real studio locations. Editorial alternating image/text rhythm
  throughout — nothing feels templated.
- **jessica-gregory-full.png** — Runway/modeling coach. Real fashion/runway photography, a
  luxury client-logo row (Chanel, Dolce & Gabbana, Givenchy, Louis Vuitton, Marc Jacobs) used as
  the trust signal instead of generic review stars, deep magenta color-block section, editorial
  black-and-white portrait. Footer explicitly credits "hand crafted by" a designer — worth
  noting because it shows even in a real market, the premium/generic gap is designer-attributable.
- **mike-ganino-full.png** — Keynote-speaking coach with a full "old Hollywood noir" theme:
  marquee-style display type, black-and-white portrait photography, a consistent red/gold/black
  palette, real press logos (Disney, Uber, TEDx, Adobe). Proof that a solo provider can commit to
  a bold, specific visual concept instead of a generic "clean coach site" look.
- **jen-carrington-full.png** — Gentle business coach. Soft editorial photography in warm,
  moody tones, color-blocked sections in dusty rose/olive/terracotta (not the usual white/navy),
  italic serif accents, real candid lifestyle photos (desk, plants, books) instead of headshots.
  Best reference for a quieter, boutique-feeling premium register.
- **marie-forleo-full.png** — Warm cream/black palette, real personality-driven photography, a
  dense grid of real celebrity-guest photos (Oprah, etc.) used as social proof instead of a logo
  row, book-launch tie-in section. Strong "media personality" register.
- **amy-porterfield-full.png** — Confident blue/pink/yellow system, multiple real professional
  portrait photos in different outfits/poses (not one static headshot reused), press-logo row
  (Forbes, Entrepreneur, Mashable), a phone mockup of her podcast, hard stat callouts (60M+
  downloads, 6,500+ reviews).
- **tony-robbins-full.png** — Mega-brand scale, but useful as an upper bound: dramatic
  color-blocked sections (purple/blue/orange), real event-crowd photography, press logos (Forbes,
  Fortune, Inc), confident percentage-stat callouts. Shows what a solo personal brand looks like
  fully scaled up.
- **matt-roberts-evolution-full.png** — Wellness/longevity positioning. Press logos, real
  facility photography, a dark "Longevity Lab" data-grid section (VO2 test, blood work, bone
  health icons), tech-partner logos (Whoop, VALD, Styku). Good reference for a
  medical-wellness-adjacent premium register.
- **mark-fisher-fitness-full.png** (now rebranded Speakeasy of Strength) — Hot pink/purple/
  graffiti aesthetic, real diverse gym-member photography, playful "3 steps" cards with
  spray-paint-style badges. Kept specifically as a counter-example to navy/black "premium" —
  proves personality-driven and fun can still read as hand-built and deliberate, not slop.
- **body-by-amanda-full.png** — Warm tan/black wellness palette, real photography throughout
  (yoga, kitchen, gym, founder portrait), clear three-tier pricing cards. Simple but consistently
  executed.
- **ikigai-integrative-full.png** — Sex therapy practice. Playful teal/pink/green organic blob
  shapes behind real photography, color-coded service cards each with real (not stock) photos,
  real founder photo with a personal, destigmatizing tone in the copy.
- **wholeness-collective-therapy-full.png** — Warm neutral editorial palette (cream, blush,
  sage), real team photography styled like a lifestyle shoot, elegant italic-script signature
  font for a founder quote, calm spa-like restraint. Best "therapist office that feels like a
  retreat" reference.

## Rejected
Scott Laidler and Shanley Cox were both walls of text/blog-style long-form pages with minimal
visual design — informative but not a design reference. The Parker Practice's domain has expired
and now shows a parked-domain ad page. Christine Hassler had several broken/empty content blocks
(blank white service cards, an empty marble section) that undercut an otherwise decent teal/purple
system.
