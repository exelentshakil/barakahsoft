# local-business-general — design reference notes

10 kept out of 16 candidates captured. Skews coffee/cafe-heavy (9 of 10) because that's where the
"real small business, genuinely premium site" hit rate was highest during sourcing — DTC boutique
retail (Caraway, Wildgrain) mostly bot-blocked or timed out rather than looked bad. Treat the
coffee examples as generally representative of "small food/beverage retail," not literally
coffee-specific — the patterns (real product photography, a confident single accent color, real
storefront/staff photos) transfer directly to bakeries, boutiques, and other local retail.

- **culture-espresso-full.png** — Best "genuinely small, hand-built, real personality" example
  in the whole library, not just this folder. Oversized serif headline over a real un-staged shop
  photo, real staff photos in branded T-shirts (not stock), confident lowercase voice ("IYKYK,
  since 2009"). Nothing about this reads templated.
- **ozone-coffee-full.png** — Custom flat-illustration style used consistently for the "Our
  Story" section (roasting/sourcing/hosting), real product photography, an interactive "Find
  Your Flavour" section with a bold yellow color block, huge bold wordmark in the footer. Best
  example of a distinct illustration system carried through a whole page.
- **colectivo-coffee-full.png** — Vintage sticker/badge illustration style, real founder photo
  with a hand-drawn ribbon graphic, retro city-skyline illustrations for each of its 3 markets
  (Milwaukee/Madison/Chicago). Extremely charming and specific — the opposite of generic.
- **felix-roasting-co-full.png** — Editorial product photography on a warm tan background,
  elegant serif wordmark, real styled flat-lay shots (coffee + book + card). Best "premium DTC
  packaged-goods" register in the folder.
- **ceremony-coffee-roasters-full.png** — Real lifestyle photography (a styled cafe-table shot
  reused intentionally in two places), a real press pull-quote section, a genuine Instagram photo
  grid (not placeholder squares), warm cream palette.
- **elementary-coffee-co-full.png** — Dense, authentic real photo grid: storefront, latte art,
  staff mid-pour, a real team photo holding product. "Crafted with love in Harrisburg, PA" — the
  photo density itself is the trust signal here.
- **panther-coffee-full.png** — Real interior photo of the actual shop (not a stock cafe photo),
  black/gold palette, a cat-silhouette logo motif used consistently (nav, footer, gift card).
- **campos-coffee-full.png** — Bold green/red system, real lifestyle photography (pouring latte,
  home brewing), a custom hand-lettered "Cherry Lane" release graphic, a real historic-building
  storefront photo. Good reference for seasonal/limited-release merchandising sections.
- **summer-moon-coffee-full.png** — Playful beach/Texas lifestyle photography, a real founder
  photo in a field, "Born & Bred" state-shaped badge as a trust signal instead of a generic
  award badge. Kept despite an empty/unloaded locations-grid section further down the page.
- **brightland-full.png** — Sunny yellow accent color, elegant serif logo, editorial olive-oil
  product photography, a founder-signature mission quote, real press-mention logos (Goop, Kitchn).
  Kept despite an intrusive discount popup covering the hero and a couple of unloaded bestseller
  thumbnails — the visible product photography and color system are strong enough to be worth the
  imperfect capture. Best non-coffee example in the folder; worth a manual recapture with the
  popup dismissed if it's used as a primary reference.

## Rejected
Ginger & White, Birch Coffee, Quill's Coffee, and Söderberg were all dropped for broken/empty
lazy-load sections (large unloaded gray or washed-out blocks) rather than on visual merit — several
had genuinely nice real photography in the parts that did render. Caraway was bot-blocked (403).
Wildgrain consistently timed out waiting for web fonts to finish loading even after a longer
timeout retry — worth a manual Browser-tool capture later if more boutique-retail (non-coffee)
examples are needed; the coffee concentration here is a real gap worth filling with a dedicated
pass on bakeries, home-goods, and clothing boutiques specifically.
